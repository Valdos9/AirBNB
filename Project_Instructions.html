STT303 · PROJECT 2 · REAL DATA · TEAMS OF 2–3

Boston on a Budget

What do Airbnb listings tell us about the price of a stay?

Your brief

Your team is preparing a data-based guide for travelers planning a week in Boston. How does listed nightly price vary with guest capacity and room type? How much could the average price vary if you inspected only a small sample of listings?

100 points · teams of 2 or 3 · 900–1,300 words · five required figures. Use techniques through Lecture 22. Suggested effort: 6–9 total person-hours including review. No multiple regression, formal hypothesis tests, confidence intervals, or scraping are required.

The supplied CSV contains all 4,368 listings and nine selected columns from Inside Airbnb’s Boston snapshot dated September 19, 2026; the listing collection spans September 19–20. These are real observations. No artificial duplicates, typos or missing values were added. Checking a condition and finding no problems is a valid result.

Our analysis focuses on private rooms or entire homes/apartments, capacity 1–6 guests, listed minimum stay 1–7 nights, and a reported nightly price greater than $0 and at most $500 USD. This is a defined travel-budget subset, not a claim that other listings are errors. A capacity value is the maximum number of guests, not the number who actually stayed. A minimum stay of seven nights or less does not guarantee availability.

1 · Clean, audit and define the study | 25 points

Read all columns as character into listings_raw to preserve long listing IDs. Remove exact duplicate rows. Create listings_clean with listing_id (from id), snapshot_date (from last_scraped), neighbourhood (from neighbourhood_cleansed), room_type, accommodates, price_usd (from price), minimum_nights, last_review and number_of_reviews.

Use string operations to squish whitespace in neighbourhood and room_type, and lowercase room_type. Set room_type as an unordered factor with levels private room, entire home/apt, shared room, hotel room, in that order. Parse currency/numbers; recode nonpositive price, capacity and minimum nights to NA. Zero reviews is valid. Parse both date columns with ymd().

Create days_since_review as numeric snapshot_date − last_review, and review_status with ordered display levels recent, older, no review date (use an unordered factor). Recent means 0–180 days inclusive; older means over 180 days. Missing last_review yields no review date; keep it missing in days_since_review. Check for future review dates and investigate if any occur.

First retain complete cases on price_usd, accommodates, minimum_nights, room_type and snapshot_date. Then apply the stated room-type, capacity, minimum-stay and price filters, in that order. Store the final result as analysis_data, drop unused room_type levels (private room stays the reference), and arrange by character listing_id. Do not remove rows solely for a missing review date. Do not add other exclusions or imputations.

Create one-row cleaning_audit with raw_rows, duplicate_rows, clean_rows, missing_price, missing_capacity, missing_minimum_nights, missing_review_date, complete_rows, room_rows, capacity_rows, stay_rows, analysis_rows. Missing counts are after recoding, before filtering. The last five row counts document the sequential filtering steps. Verify unique IDs, date ranges, observed labels and nonnegative review ages. Explain missingness and the difference between an invalid value and a valid listing outside the study scope.

2 · Explore the budget listings | 20 points

Using analysis_data throughout, create room_summary with room_type, n, mean_price, median_price, sd_price and review_summary with review_status, n, mean_price. Produce (1) a price histogram with binwidth $25, (2) a price-by-room-type boxplot, and (3) a capacity-versus-price scatterplot with one overall linear trend, no confidence ribbon and transparent points. Label prices as USD per night and capacity as guests.

Compare mean and median, shape and spread, room categories and review-status groups. Identify an unusual observation or explain the visible tail; do not label it a data error without evidence. Explain why missing review dates and review recency cannot directly measure demand. Mention the upper-price cutoff when interpreting the distribution.

3 · Capacity and price: simple regression | 20 points

Compute capacity_cor and fit capacity_model: price_usd ~ accommodates. Create capacity_terms using broom::tidy() and keep term, estimate. Create capacity_augmented using broom::augment(). Use predict() to create capacity_predictions with accommodates values 2, 4, 6 and predicted_price. Produce (4) residuals versus fitted values with a zero reference line.

Write the fitted equation. Interpret slope and intercept in context; is zero capacity observed? Explain the four-guest prediction as an estimated mean listed price for listings of that capacity, not a guaranteed booking quote. For the first analysis_data row, interpret observed price, fitted price and residual. Assess the residual plot, including the repeated capacity values and $500 cutoff. Explain why the association is not evidence that increasing a property’s guest limit causes its price to rise by the estimated slope.

4 · Private room or entire place? | 15 points

Fit a separate room_model: price_usd ~ room_type with private room as reference. Create room_terms with term, estimate using tidy(), and room_predictions with room_type, predicted_price using predict(), ordered private room then entire home/apt. Verify that predictions equal the corresponding room_summary means.

Interpret the intercept and the entire-home coefficient. Distinguish the difference in means from the entire-home mean itself. Explain why these unadjusted comparisons do not hold guest capacity, location or amenities constant. Avoid causal claims or claiming an individual traveler will necessarily save the estimated difference.

5 · How much can a small sample mislead? | 15 points

Treat analysis_data$price_usd as a finite teaching population and compute population_mean. Set seed 30322; draw 1,000 simple random samples of size 20 without replacement within each sample, followed by 1,000 of size 80. Samples from separate repetitions can overlap. Use a loop or replicate(); do not refit regressions. Store sampling_means with sample_size, mean_price (2,000 rows) and sampling_summary with sample_size, mean_of_means, sd_of_means.

Produce (5) faceted histograms of the sample means with shared axes, binwidth $5, and a vertical line at population_mean. Compare centers and spreads, distinguish individual-price SD from sample-mean SD, and explain what a seed does. Explain why this exercise describes variability within the filtered snapshot, not uncertainty about all Boston listings or future prices. This is repeated sampling without replacement, not bootstrap resampling.
